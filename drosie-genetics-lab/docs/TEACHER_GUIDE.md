# Teacher Guide (K–12)

Drosie Genetics Lab is a free browser simulator. Students breed virtual *Drosophila melanogaster*, predict offspring, and analyze simulated data. No accounts are needed, and progress stays in the student's browser.

**Important framing for students.** Every offspring count is *simulated* from the rules of inheritance. The counts are not real laboratory measurements, so random variation is real but the flies are not. The app labels all results "SIMULATED".

## Quick start for a lesson

1. Open the lab and choose the level (top bar): **Little** (5–8), **Junior** (9–12), **Researcher** (13–17) or **University**.
2. Open the **Challenge Board** and click a mission → *Start this mission*. Progress shows in the top bar.
3. Students work through the stations: **Stock Center → Breeding → Microscope → Data → Notebook**.
4. For classroom display, turn on *Settings → Text size 130%*. *Reduce motion* and *Read dialogue aloud* are also available.

**Reproducible class data:** give every group the same **seed** (Breeding Station). The same parents, seed and number of offspring always produce identical counts. Different seeds give the class a spread of results to compare.

## Standards alignment (only where it genuinely applies)

| Level | Standard | How the lab addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| Little (grade 1) | NGSS **1-LS3-1**: young animals are like, but not exactly like, their parents | Missions L2, L3: predict, breed, compare offspring with parents |
| Little/Junior (grade 3) | NGSS **3-LS3-1**: analyze data for evidence that traits are inherited and that variation exists | L3, J1, J3: picture tallies, class data comparison |
| Junior (grades 6–8) | NGSS **MS-LS3-2**: sexual reproduction results in genetic variation | J1, J2: segregation, Punnett squares, F2 variation |
| Researcher (HS) | NGSS **HS-LS3-1** (DNA/chromosomes code for traits), **HS-LS3-2** (new combinations through meiosis), **HS-LS3-3** (statistics and probability explain trait distributions) | Chromosome Explorer; R1–R5 (X-linkage, independent assortment, chi-square) |
| Researcher (HS, Texas) | TEKS Biology §112.42 **BIO.8.B**: predict outcomes using monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, including sex-linked traits | R1 (sex-linked), R3 (dihybrid), J2 (monohybrid) |

The university missions are not mapped to K–12 standards. Note that NGSS HS-LS3 assessment boundaries exclude the phases of meiosis; the Chromosome Explorer shows meiosis outcomes, not phases.

## Lesson plans

### Lesson 1 — Traits and observation (Little Geneticist, 30 min)
- **Objectives:** Observe and describe traits; compare two organisms.
- **Do:** Mission L1 at the Microscope. Students zoom on the Everyday fly and the Snowy-eyed fly and describe the difference.
- **Discuss:** "What else could be different between two flies?" (wings, body color).
- **Assessment:** L1 question; students draw one fly and label its eyes, wings and body.

### Lesson 2 — Predict, then test (Little Geneticist, 30 min)
- **Do:** Mission L2. Students make a prediction *before* breeding two tiny-wing flies, then compare babies with parents.
- **Then:** Mission L3 (Dark × Everyday fly). All the babies look tan. Ask: "Is the dark color gone forever?" Leave this as an open question for the next lesson.
- **Caution:** Don't tell children every trait is "dominant or recessive". The app avoids that language at this level.

### Lesson 3 — Mendel with flies (Junior, 45–60 min)
- **Objectives:** Define gene and allele; explain dominant/recessive; describe a 3:1 ratio.
- **Do:** Mission J1. P cross vestigial × wild type → keep an F1 female and male → F1 × F1 with ≥ 100 offspring.
- **Discuss:** Why did vestigial disappear and come back? Why isn't the result exactly 75%?
- **Exit ticket:** Worksheet 2, questions 1–3.

### Lesson 4 — Punnett squares and probability (Junior, 45 min)
- **Do:** Mission J2 in **Prediction mode**. Students enter fractions, check them, then breed to test.
- **Extend:** Mission J3. Run the same cross with 20 and with 1,000 offspring, pool the class data on the board, and discuss sampling variation.

### Lesson 5 — Morgan's white-eyed fly (Researcher, 60 min)
- **Do:** Missions R1 and R2. Split results by sex at the Data computer.
- **Key idea:** Males are *hemizygous* for X-linked genes. Reciprocal crosses differ for X-linked genes but match for autosomal ones.
- **Assessment:** Worksheet 3.

### Lesson 6 — Two genes and chi-square (Researcher, 2 × 50 min)
- **Do:** Missions R3 and R5. Students test F2 data against 9:3:3:1.
- **Emphasize:** p > 0.05 means *consistent with* the hypothesis, **not** proof.
- **Assessment:** Worksheet 4.

### Lesson 7 — Testcross detective (Researcher, 40 min)
- **Do:** Mission R4. Each student has a hidden mystery-female genotype, stored per browser.
- **Discuss:** One vestigial offspring proves heterozygosity, but zero vestigial in n offspring only makes it unlikely, at (½)ⁿ.

## Common misconceptions the lab addresses

- *"Dominant means more common."* Missions show recessive alleles hiding in carriers.
- *"A Punnett square tells you exactly what you'll get."* J3 and class seed comparisons show sampling variation.
- *"Y makes flies male."* Not in *Drosophila*; see Drosie's facts and the Chromosome Explorer.
- *"p > 0.05 proves the model."* The interpretation text never says this.
- *"Black and ebony are the same gene."* They are different genes on chromosomes 2 and 3.
