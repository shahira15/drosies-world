Free fruit fly lesson plans and printable science worksheets

Everything teachers need to bring Drosophila biology into a K–8 classroom: seven printable lesson activity sheets, interactive games for stations or whole-class demos, discussion questions, and a genetics simulator with teacher guides for older students. All resources are free, run in a web browser and do not ask children for personal information.

Drosie’s First Scientific Conference

A free, ready-to-use research-communication unit. Learners investigate a simulated fruit fly experiment or their own project, then write an abstract, build a poster and practise presenting. The teacher and parent guide covers everything you need to run it.

  • Learning objectives and discussion questions for every chapter
  • Time estimates (not yet timed with learners) and session plans
  • Assessment ideas, privacy and accessibility notes
  • Guided and independent pathways, including original student research

Which resource fits which grade?

Age ranges are suggestions based on each resource's content and reading demand; you know your students best. Ages 5–8 roughly correspond to kindergarten through grade 2 or 3 in the United States.

Kindergarten and first grade (ages 5–6)

Start with the storybook read aloud, then Lesson 1 and Lesson 2 to sequence the life cycle. The Life Cycle Adventure works well on a shared screen with the class calling out the next stage.

Second grade (ages 7–8)

Add the Secret Fly Lab for microscope and safety skills, Lesson 3 on trait variation, and Lesson 6 for predictions and simple data.

Grades 3–5 (ages 8–11)

Use the life cycle activities page (including its older-learner extensions on temperature and metamorphosis), the virtual fly lab and the Climbing Challenge to practise predicting, testing and explaining.

Ages 9–12 (roughly grades 4–7)

Try Drosie’s First Scientific Conference to practise research communication: reading data honestly, writing an abstract and presenting a poster. Learners can follow the simulated fruit fly investigation or bring their own project. See the teacher and parent guide for timing, discussion questions and assessment ideas.

Standards: the Drosie Genetics Lab teacher guide lists NGSS and Texas TEKS connections for its missions. The other resources have not been formally aligned to standards, so please check them against your own curriculum.

Using them in class

  • Stations: one group on a game, one with a printable sheet, one reading the storybook.
  • Predict, test, explain: students write a prediction before each simulation and compare it with the result.
  • Whole-class demo: project a game and let students vote on each decision.
  • Home extension: share a Letters from Drosie link with families.

For older students: genetics lessons

The Drosie Genetics Lab has levels from ages 5–8 through university. Its teacher and instructor resources include a K–12 teacher guide with lesson plans and answer keys, an undergraduate instructor guide, printable worksheets and a scientific reference listing every modeled gene with FlyBase sources.

Real science and simulations

Drosie's World separates what is simulated from what is real. The games and virtual labs are simulations: their outcomes are generated by the programs for learning and are not real experiment data. Descriptions of fruit fly biology and historical discoveries are drawn from published sources, listed on the Fruit Fly Biology page. We do not provide a guide for rearing live flies; if you keep live animals, follow your school's policies.

Using Drosie in your classroom or research? The book can be cited as: Arzoo, S. (2026). Drosie, the Fruit Fly (Version 1.0). Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23550876 Citations for every resource, in APA, MLA, Chicago, BibTeX and RIS, are on the Cite Drosie's World page.
