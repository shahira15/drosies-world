Can fruit flies climb?

Fruit flies often climb up after they are tapped down. Scientists call this negative geotaxis (moving away from gravity's pull).

  1. 1 Guess how many of 10 flies will climb past the orange line.
  2. 2 Watch for 10 seconds.
  3. 3 Write down what happened. Do it 3 times!
What is a climbing assay?

A climbing assay is a simple lab test. Scientists put flies in a vial, tap them to the bottom, and count how many climb past a marked line in a set amount of time. Real labs use standardized conditions and decide the observation time ahead of time.

This is a simulation. The flies are drawings that move by a simple computer rule. These are not real lab results, and they can't tell us anything about real flies.

Think like a scientist

Did every trial give the same number? Why might that be?

Why do scientists repeat an experiment?

One trial can be a lucky or unlucky sample. Several trials show a pattern, and how much the results vary. A single result isn't enough to be sure.

If you compared two groups of flies, what would you keep the same?

The same vial, the same timing, the same temperature and light, and the same way of tapping the flies down, with many repeats. Even then, this simulation cannot show anything about real flies. It only practises how to observe and record.
