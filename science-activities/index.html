✳ BIG IDEA

Why do scientists study fruit flies?

Fruit flies (Drosophila melanogaster) are tiny, easy to keep and grow up fast. At about 25°C a fruit fly goes from egg to adult in roughly 9–10 days, so scientists can watch many generations in a short time.

Many fruit fly genes have counterparts in other animals, including people, so what researchers learn about how flies grow, move, age and inherit traits helps them ask better questions about biology in general. Read real examples in the Discovery Library.

Start here if you are new

  1. Read the storybook together.
  2. Follow the life cycle, then run the Climbing Challenge.
  3. Try the Secret Fly Lab (5–8) or the Genetics Lab (older students).
✳ FOR TEACHERS & PARENTS

Using these activities in class or at home

Classroom ideas

  • Station rotation: one group on the life cycle game, one on the Climbing Challenge, one with a printable sheet.
  • Predict, test, explain: have students write a prediction before each simulation, then compare it to what happened.
  • Whole-class demo: project the Climbing Challenge or the Genetics Lab microscope and let students call out predictions.
  • Home extension: send the storybook and a Letters from Drosie link home for family reading.

Good to know

  • Games run in the browser. Drosie's World does not ask children for names, emails or accounts.
  • Climbing and grow-your-own activities are simulations, not real measurements or live animals.
  • Age ranges on this page are suggestions based on each resource's content. You know your students best. Time needed varies, since most activities are self-paced.

Discussion questions

  • Which stage of the life cycle looks the most different from the adult? What might that stage be doing?
  • Why did we run three trials instead of one? What changed between trials?
  • What does your prediction say, and what would change your mind?
  • Which traits did the offspring share with each parent? Which were different?
  • Why might scientists choose a small animal that grows quickly for their experiments?
  • What is one thing a simulation can show well, and one thing it cannot?
